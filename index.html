22Why Does Time Speed Up As You Age?

At age 10, one year is 10% of your life. At age 50, it's 2%. Your brain encodes time proportionally to lived experience — a phenomenon called "proportional theory." Additionally, as we age, the brain processes fewer new experiences (novelty), and novel experiences are what create distinct memory markers. Routine collapses time. The brain also physically slows — neural processing speed decreases, and fewer new neural connections form, meaning fewer "frames" are captured per unit of time. Children's brains capture vastly more sensory frames per second than adult brains.
Practice: Seek novelty daily — new routes, new foods, new skills, travel. Break routine deliberately. Mindfulness and presence expand subjective time by increasing frame density. The Science tab itself is an act of learning that slows perceived time.
